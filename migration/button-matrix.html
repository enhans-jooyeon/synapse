Button — full variant matrix

Every axis of the Button crossed, rendered from the shipped --sy-* tokens rather than hard-coded hexes — so this page fails if a token moves. The variant space itself is closed and complete at 12 cells (4 buttonStyle × 3 target); what the matrix is for is finding combinations the spec does not resolve. Those are collected in §6.

3 · Size × content

4 × 5 per style = 80

Radius is size-relative (xs→6, sm→8, md→10, lg→12; xs moved 4→6 on 2026-07-31 — see the decision aid above). Icons are 12 at xs and 16 at sm/md/lg; labels are 11 / 13 / 13 / 14. Resolved 2026-07-31 — the spec did specify icon 12 at xs and the render was inheriting the global 16 (a real spec↔render drift, now fixed); lg moved to label 16 / icon 20 so a heroes-only button holds its own beside a heading-xl title. Icon-only cells are square: width = the size's control height.

xs radius — decision aid

resolved · 6px shipped

The complaint: xs reads squarer than the rest. It measures out: radius÷height is 0.167 at xs versus a tight 0.250 / 0.278 / 0.300 band at sm/md/lg — xs sits 33% below the bottom of the band, so it is the only size off the progression. 6px lands on 0.250 (matching sm exactly); 8px reaches 0.333, which would make xs proportionally the roundest size of the four, past lg. 6 is not on the radius scale, so option 1 was a scale addition (precedent: radius.10 was added off the 4px scale for the same optical reason). Rendered at the real xs height (24) and padding (8). Ruled 2026-07-31: 6px. Shipped as an internal primitive.radius.6 exposed through radius.control-xs → --sy-radius-control-xs, mirroring the radius.10/control-md shape. radius.xs (4) was left alone — it has 27 other consumers (checkbox and small-inset corners), so this changed Button's mapping, not the primitive.

5 · Pill option

primary only · Guided heroes

radius.full override, permitted for primary in Guided-archetype heroes and empty-state first-use. Never in forms, toolbars, tables or dense regions.

Undefined: the spec says the pill option applies to primary in heroes but does not say whether target=brand may take it — a pill AI CTA is plausible (Composer send) yet also the most scarcity-governed control in the system. And no size is named; lg is shown because heroes are the stated context.

6 · Coverage audit

all 12 ruled0 open+3 ruled 2026-07-31

What rendering the whole matrix exposed, and how each was decided on 2026-07-30 (plus three size-ladder rulings on 07-31, at the end). The variant space itself was never incomplete — it is closed at 12 cells. These were combinations the spec did not resolve, which is what actually causes two engineers to build the same button differently. Three turned out to be defects rather than omissions: coloured outline buttons had no hover at all, the focus-ring rule failed 1.4.11 in 11 of 12 cells, and control-height-xs existed in the CSS but not the JSON.

`outline × destructive` / `× brand` hoverruledCLOSED — the border steps. These two cells previously had no hover state at all, the only functional defect in the set. Now border.error → border.error-hover (red.500→600, ΔE 10.1) and action.brand-border → action.brand-border-hover (azure.500→600, ΔE 18.5) — both exceed the ΔE 8.6 the default step already shipped with. Two new tokens; dark mode steps lighter. Chosen over tinting the fill on axis grounds: buttonStyle owns behaviour, target owns hue, so target must never change the interaction model.
Focus-ring colourruledCLOSED — keyed to target, full strength. Measuring the old §9 rule (a ~50% color-mix of the button's own colour) showed it failed WCAG 1.4.11 in 11 of 12 cells — secondary × default at 1.04:1 and outline × default at 1.10:1, effectively invisible rings on the two most-used variants. A lightened tint of an already-light fill can never reach 3:1, so the clause is deleted. Rings now: default → border.focus (5.21/9.58), destructive → border.error-hover (6.19/7.75), brand → action.brand-border-hover (6.19/6.42) — all three gated. Independent of style, so all four styles in a target share one ring. Amended 2026-07-30: the default ring is NEUTRAL — border.focus-input #33333A, 12.53:1. Higher measured contrast than the indigo it replaces, yet quieter to look at: at a 2px band chroma pulls the eye harder than luminance, and a saturated band had no fill weight to balance it on outline and ghost. It also settles §1.1's own claim that the primary action colour is black, not blue. destructive and brand keep hue — the two intents worth spending attention on, and rare by construction. Further amended 2026-07-30: strength now comes from buttonStyle. primary is near-black so a strong dark ring sits naturally on it; the same ring around a #F4F4F6 tonal fill or a transparent ghost is darker than anything else in the control and stops reading as part of the button. secondary/outline/ghost therefore take soft rings — the 500 step of each family (#83838D / #D2403E / #0073E6), all three mode-invariant because a mid-tone clears 1.4.11 against both pages (3.75/5.30, 4.62/4.30, 4.57/4.35). azure.400 was rejected for the soft brand ring at 3.10:1 light — too little margin. Hue still comes from target, so intent survives; only the weight changes.
Coloured `ghost` hoverruledCLOSED — stays neutral. All four ghost cells take the bg.hover alpha overlay; hue lives in the label. bg.hover is the universal hover across rows, menu items, nav and chips, and ghost is the button most like those. Decisive detail: a tinted ghost hover would be byte-identical to secondary × target at rest (#FDF1F0, #F1F7FE), collapsing the lowest and standard tiers into each other. Label contrast on the composited surface holds at 5.54/5.68/5.67:1.
Icon size at `xs`ruledCLOSED — 12 added to the icon scale. This exposed a contradiction rather than a gap: foundations §8 said 16/20/24 and no other sizes, while components.md already specified 12px icons in Badge and SourceChip and preview.html carried 92 off-scale renders. 12 is now the metadata step and xs icons take it; 10/11/14/18 are explicitly off-scale. The 20 step remains unassigned to any control height.
Horizontal padding per sizeruledCLOSED — ladder 8/10/12/16. Padding was the only Button dimension that did not step (height, radius and label size all did), leaving xs at a padding:height ratio of 0.50 and lg at 0.30; the ladder brings all four to 0.31–0.40 and md is unchanged. Applying it caught a second bug: control-height-xs shipped in the CSS but was never in the JSON, so the canonical source did not describe a size the system renders. Recovered.
`loading` on an icon-only buttonruledCLOSED — the spinner replaces the glyph. Width is preserved trivially since the button is a fixed square. Because the affordance vanishes while busy, aria-label MUST persist unchanged with aria-busy added alongside — never relabelled to Loading. The spinner is the registry loader-2 glyph rotated by CSS; reduced-motion stops the rotation and never swaps the icon.
`pill` × target and × sizeruledCLOSED — lg only; default and brand, never destructive. Pill and lg are both hero-scoped so they travel together. brand is allowed because a first-run Ask-the-agent hero is the most plausible pill context in the product, and the combination is self-limiting — it already stacks max-1-brand-per-screen with hero-only-pill. destructive is incoherent at hero scale: solid destructive is confirmation-only and dialogs are never Guided heroes.
Leading + trailing icon togetherruledCONFIRMED legal — now explicitly, not by omission. Self-limiting: leading icons are permitted only in AI-entry or toolbar/filter contexts, so the combination is reachable only in a toolbar, where icon + label + chevron-down is a filter dropdown and the leading glyph is exactly the scanning aid that permission exists to provide. Both padding trims apply (10/10 at md). The narrow ban stands: never two chevrons.
`ghost` gains a fill when disabledruledCONFIRMED — and a sibling contradiction fixed. The grey box is intended: bg.disabled in every variant is the single unambiguous disabled signal. Checking it revealed ToggleButton disabled with text.disabled and no fill — the only control in the system doing so, at 2.12:1 with nothing framing it. ToggleButton now matches. Announce to QA: shadcn and old Synapse both left disabled ghosts transparent, so this is the migration's most visible single appearance change.
Disabled label colourruledREVERSED 2026-07-30 — one token in all 12 cells. The per-target disabled labels made disabled buttons look available: action.brand-fg-disabled was azure.500, the exact same hex as the live brand fill, and action.danger-fg-disabled was red.400, one step off the live danger fill — so both ran at HIGHER contrast on the disabled fill (4.16:1, 3.63:1) than the neutral label (2.12:1). Both tokens deleted. The reasoning that produced them was wrong on inspection: a disabled control has no need to signal intent, because intent only matters to someone who can invoke it, and the label text already says which action it is.
No `active`/pressed stateruledDROPPED 2026-07-30. Buttons have no third fill. The state had already been reversed twice in one day — first “identical to hover by design”, then a real step — and the second reversal made the underlying point clearly: pressed feedback buys about one frame, on a control the cursor is already resting on, whose hover state has already confirmed it is live. Three tokens went with it (action.primary-bg-active, action.brand-bg-active, status.danger-bg-solid-active). bg.active survives — it was never a Button token: table rows and menu items use it, where the pointer crosses a large target and the confirmation does earn its keep. The state axis in §1 and §2 is now 5 states, not 6.
`xs` is inline-onlyruledNot a gap but the easiest rule to break: permitted only under the WCAG 2.5.8 Inline exception. Every xs cell rendered here outside a sentence context would be a violation in production. Shown for completeness, not as a licence.

Ruled 2026-07-31 — the size ladder

1. xs radius 4 → 6px. xs was the only size off the radius÷height progression — 0.167 against a 0.250 / 0.278 / 0.300 band — so it read squarer rather than proportionally smaller. 6px lands on 0.250, matching sm. Reusing radius.sm (8) was rejected at 0.333: it would have made the smallest control proportionally the roundest, past lg. Second sanctioned off-4px-scale control step, after radius.10.

2. lg type — explored and REVERTED; the ladder is unchanged. The starting objection was reasonable: the spec assigns lg to heroes only, "beside heading-xl+ titles", yet its label sits just 1px above the sm/md default. Label 16 / icon 20 was built and then walked back in two steps — the icon first (a 20px icon beside a 16px label inverts the conventional icon ≈ label pairing, and strokes filling a box carry more weight than text at the same nominal size), then the label, once benchmarking showed 14px is the industry norm for button labels (Carbon productive, Ant, Material 3 Label Large, shadcn/ui). Final: label 14, icon 16 — as before. lg takes its presence from height, padding and radius, not type. Worth keeping: 20/40 satisfied the 0.50 icon ratio that sm/xs share, so the ratio flagged nothing — ratios catch outliers, they do not predict perceived weight. sm and md deliberately keep the same 13px label — that is the dense range where a changing type size costs more scannability than a changing height does; they separate by height, padding and radius instead. Practice across systems is split (Carbon and shadcn/ui hold one label size across sizes; Ant, Chakra, Radix and Material's newer sizes scale it), and the split tracks density — so scaling only at the ends is the coherent position for a dense enterprise system.

3. xs icon 16 → 12px — this one was a defect, not a decision. components.md already specified icon 12 at xs; the render inherited the global 16px .icon size and nothing compared the two, so the spec and the render disagreed silently. This matrix previously recorded the 12px value as "my inference, not the spec" — the inference was right and the spec was already explicit. No gate covers rendered geometry against spec prose, which is the same class of gap SY020 closed for token values.
